01Entertainment

Turning Global Titles Into Local Wins

Netflix — Zebra Stripes Networks

The Challenge

Netflix's biggest titles — Bridgerton, Squid Game, Money Heist — launched with strong global campaigns, but African audiences were being reached through the same generic, one-size-fits-all content. Regional visibility was lagging behind the titles' actual cultural relevance in the market.

The Approach

Rebuilt the launch model around the region instead of adapting it after the fact — integrating PR, SEO, paid media, and audience analytics into a single localized go-to-market framework. On-the-ground event coordination and targeted media relations turned press interest into real regional buzz ahead of each release, while stakeholder management across creative, legal, and business teams kept content creation and executive messaging moving in lockstep with tight launch timelines.

02Financial Services

Giving Leadership a Voice the Market Trusted

Stanbic Bank — Zebra Stripes Networks

The Challenge

Stanbic Bank's senior leadership had the strategy and the credibility, but not a consistent public voice — executive perspectives weren't reaching stakeholders in a way that built visible trust or reinforced the bank's market position.

The Approach

Built an executive communications program from the ground up — speeches, thought-leadership content, and briefing materials designed to put leadership directly in front of stakeholders, backed by a quarterly speaking cadence that kept executives visible year-round rather than only during major moments. Recognizing a widening gap with younger consumers, built tailored market growth strategies and coordinated event activations aimed squarely at a youthful audience — closing a disconnect that generic corporate messaging on its own couldn't reach.

04Luxury Spirits

Protecting Exclusivity While Growing Reach

Martell Cognac "Martell Swift" (Pernod Ricard) — Notabene

The Challenge

Martell's cognac audience expects refinement, not volume — mass-market marketing tactics that work for other consumer brands risk cheapening a luxury brand's positioning. Compounding the challenge, a competitor held a strong, entrenched position in the cognac category, meaning the Martell Swift campaign had to grow market share in a space where audience loyalty was already spoken for — without resorting to the volume-first tactics that would undercut the brand's exclusivity.

The Approach

Built an experiential-led strategy centered on curated brand moments and selective media and influencer partnerships — prioritizing the right audience over the widest audience, with storytelling calibrated to a premium tone throughout. Market growth strategy was built to chip away at the competitor's hold gradually, through exclusivity and cultural relevance rather than head-on price or volume competition.

06Production Company

One Consistent Voice, On Set and Off

Inkblot Productions

The Challenge

As a production company, attention and urgency inside the business naturally gathered around active shoots and release timelines — internal systems and governance work, like a SharePoint rollout, risked being ignored entirely if it competed for attention with what was happening on set. At the same time, the company's external-facing presence — media relations, stakeholder messaging, and public-facing content — needed to stay just as consistent as the internal side, even as production schedules shifted around both.

The Approach

Ran internal and external communications as one coordinated function rather than two separate efforts. Internally, modernized the intranet and translated SharePoint governance standards into clear, adoptable communication for staff. Externally, managed media relations and stakeholder messaging through crisis moments and shifting timelines, while producing branded external content — newsletters, infographics, blog content — that kept the company's public voice steady regardless of what was happening behind the scenes.

Range of Work

Intranet & website management (WordPress, GoDaddy)
Governance messaging for SharePoint rollout
External media relations
Branded external content (newsletters, infographics, blogs)
